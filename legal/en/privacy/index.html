VitaPass privacy policy

Version 1.1 · 15 October 2026 · Draft pending legal review and review by the data protection officer. The Spanish version prevails.

1. The essentials

  • Your health data is encrypted on your phone before it leaves it. We store it, but we cannot read it.
  • You decide who sees what: each profession can only receive what corresponds to it, and you approve every access.
  • We do not sell or share your data, there is no advertising, no third-party analytics, and we do not use your data to train artificial intelligence.
  • You can download or delete everything from the app at any time, and withdraw each consent as easily as you gave it.

3. What data we process

Account data

Email address, password (only its encrypted hash), second factor if you enable it, and sign-up and sign-in dates.

Identification data (in clear on the server)

First name, surnames, date of birth, sex and your VitaPass code, which is random and not derived from any other data. We keep them unencrypted because they are needed for the service to work: so a relative or a doctor knows who they are accepting, so the date of birth decides who manages a minor's record, and so sex allows the family tree to be inferred.

Identity document (optional)

If you provide it, we do not store the number: only a fingerprint (HMAC-SHA256 with a secret key kept separately) to prevent duplicate accounts.

Health data (end-to-end encrypted)

Everything you put in your record: allergies, blood group, medication, health problems, operations, implants, vaccines, vital signs, lab results, reports and their photos or PDFs, occupational health checks, family history, advance directives, health cards (including the social security number), emergency contacts and, if you use a home testing device, its measurements. They are encrypted on your device with a key that only your devices and your recovery key have. On the server we only see how many reports you store and how much space they take.

Some categories are treated as especially sensitive (mental health, HIV and sexually transmitted infections, sexual and reproductive health, abortion, addictions and genetics): they never go out by default in a QR or to your family.

Data about relationships with other people

The family relationships both of you accept (including, only if you say so, whether they are biological), who manages whose record (guardianships, with the supporting document for a dependent adult), your family doctor and the sections you let them see, which relatives to alert in an emergency, and the authorisations you give professionals (to whom, when and which sections; the content is encrypted).

Access log

Every time someone opens your QR or your record we store: who they said they were or which verified professional they were, their profession and registration number, date and time, their browser language, the sections viewed and their truncated IP address (never the full address). You can see it in the app.

Alerts and emergencies

If someone opens your emergency QR or you press SOS, we store that it happened, when, and who was alerted. Whatever the health professional or your phone adds (where you are, a phone number, a note and the location) is sealed for each relative: we cannot read it.

Health professionals' data

Profession, professional body, registration number, verification status and dates, and the accesses they make to patients' records.

Technical data

Your phone's notification token and its system (iOS or Android), the usage log of AI reading (date and number of characters, never the text), the SOS alerts sent and the Apple Wallet card signatures (to limit abuse), and the technical logs of our hosting provider (which include connection IP addresses).

What we do not process

We do not read your contacts. There is no background location: location is only obtained when you press SOS. We use no analytics or advertising SDKs and no tracking cookies. We do not record audio.

4. Purposes and legal bases

Keeping your health record (storing it, syncing it between your devices, translating it and exporting it). Basis: your explicit consent to the processing of health data (art. 6.1.a and 9.2.a GDPR), which you give when signing up.

Showing the emergency summary with the QR, the sticker, the lock-screen wallpaper or the Wallet card, also if you are unconscious. Basis: your explicit consent when you create the QR (art. 9.2.a) and, if you cannot give it at that moment, the protection of your vital interests (art. 6.1.d and 9.2.c).

Sharing with a professional the part that corresponds to their profession and that you approve, or continuously with your family doctor. Basis: your explicit consent in each authorisation (art. 9.2.a). The professional processes what they receive as an independent controller, to provide you with care and under professional secrecy (art. 9.2.h and 9.3).

Family: relationships, shared family history and emergency alerts to the relatives you choose. Basis: the consent of both parties (art. 9.2.a), and vital interests when we alert them in an emergency (art. 9.2.c).

Records of minors and dependants. Basis: the consent of their parent or legal representative (art. 7 of Spanish Organic Law 3/2018, LOPDGDD) and, from age 14, also the minor's consent for what they can decide themselves.

Reading reports with cloud AI, only when you ask for it for a specific report. Basis: your explicit consent for that report (art. 9.2.a). The transfer to the US is covered by the European Commission's standard contractual clauses (art. 46.2.c) [PENDING: sign the contract with Anthropic].

SOS button: calling 112, alerting the relatives you chose and sending them your location, only when you press it. Basis: your consent when you press it (art. 9.2.a) and the protection of your vital interests (art. 6.1.d and 9.2.c). The location is sealed for each relative: we cannot read it.

Emergency card in Apple Wallet, only if you add it. Basis: your consent when creating the QR (art. 9.2.a). Only a list of fingerprints reaches our server so that we can sign it.

Managing your account and its security (sign-up, sign-in, recovery, notifications, access log, abuse prevention). Basis: performance of the contract you accept with the terms (art. 6.1.b) and our legal obligation to protect the data (art. 6.1.c and 32).

Verifying health professionals with their professional body. Basis: the contract with the professional (art. 6.1.b) and our legitimate interest in preventing anyone from posing as a health professional to see patients' data (art. 6.1.f).

Keeping proof of your consents (what you accepted, which version and when). Basis: our legal obligation to be able to demonstrate it (art. 7.1 GDPR).

We make no automated decisions that affect you (art. 22), we do no profiling, we give no medical advice and we use the data for nothing other than what this list says.

5. Who we share it with

With whoever you decide

The health professional who scans your QR, the professionals whose access you approve, your family doctor and the relatives you accept. Each receives only their part, encrypted for them.

Providers who work for us

They are processors, with a contract under art. 28 GDPR. None of them sees readable health data, except Anthropic with the text of the report you send it, and only to read it at that moment. You can ask us for a copy of the safeguards for each transfer outside the EU by writing to the privacy email.

  • Supabase Inc. (database, sign-in, storage of encrypted reports and server functions). Servers in Frankfurt (Germany). It sees account and identification data in clear and everything else encrypted. Its parent company is in the US: the transfer is covered by the European Commission's standard contractual clauses [PENDING: confirm against the Supabase contract].
  • Transactional email (confirming your email and recovering your password): currently Supabase's email service; [PENDING: own email provider]. It receives your email address and the content of the message, which never contains health data.
  • Push notifications: Expo (650 Industries, Inc.), Apple and Google. To alert you in an emergency, the notification goes through Expo and through Apple's (iPhone) or Google's (Android) service. It carries your relative's first name and the type of alert ("Urgent alert about Ana"), never health data or location. These are transfers to the US covered by the EU-US Data Privacy Framework or standard contractual clauses.
  • Anthropic, PBC (reading reports with AI, only if you ask for it for a report). It receives the text of that report with your name, document, phone numbers and emails blacked out on your phone beforehand, without any identifier of yours. It processes it without storing it and without using it for training (zero retention). Transfer to the US covered by standard contractual clauses.
  • GitHub, Inc. (Microsoft) hosts the page that opens QR codes and the account deletion page. It receives the IP address of visitors, like any website. The QR data never reaches it: the key goes in the part of the address that the browser does not send.
  • Expo (650 Industries, Inc.) also serves the app updates, and therefore receives your phone's IP address and app version.

Apple Wallet

If you add the emergency card to Apple Wallet, the card is built on your iPhone: our server only receives a list of fingerprints to sign it. The card stays in your Wallet and in your iCloud backup, under Apple's terms.

Outside the European Union

If a health professional in another country opens your QR, it is you who decides to show them your data so they can treat you (art. 49.1.a GDPR), or it is done to protect your life if you are unconscious (art. 49.1.f). That professional is not subject to the GDPR: we remind you of this when you create the QR.

Nobody else

We do not sell, rent or give your data to anyone, not even "anonymised", nor to employers, insurers or advertisers. We would only hand it over to an authority with a legal order, and what we hold about your health is encrypted and we cannot read it.

If you are the healthcare professional opening a QR

When you open a VitaPass QR we record, so that the patient knows who has seen their record: what you type about who you are (or your profession and registration number if you are verified), the date and time, your browser's language, the sections viewed and your truncated IP address. Basis: our legitimate interest in giving the patient control over their record (art. 6.1.f). It is kept while the patient's account exists. You have the rights in section 8 by writing to the privacy email.

6. How long we keep it

  • For as long as you have the account. We do not delete records for inactivity, because a health record may be important years later.
  • Access log: while the account exists, so that you can see who has looked at your record. It is deleted with the account.
  • Consultation QR codes and authorisations to professionals: expire after 24 hours by default and 7 days at most.
  • When you delete the account, everything is deleted immediately: your record, your reports, your keys, your QR codes (they stop giving up-to-date data instantly), your relationships and what your relatives received from you, your alerts and your access log. Our provider's backups disappear within 35 days at most.
  • Proof of consents: when you delete the account we keep, blocked, only the record of what you accepted and when (without any health data or direct identification) for 3 years, in case a court or the data protection authority asks for it (art. 32 LOPDGDD). It is then deleted automatically.
  • What a professional has already seen and noted in their own clinical record is kept by them, under their responsibility.

7. Minors and dependants

  • Under 14: their record is created and kept by their parent or guardian, from their account.
  • From 14: the minor gives their own consent and creates their own account; their parents can no longer create a new record for them. If the parents created it earlier, the minor can move it to their account with a transfer code.   • From 16: the minor is in control (age of medical consent in Spain); the guardian's access ends unless the minor keeps it.
  • If both parents keep the record, any change in what is shared is notified to both.
  • Managing the record of a dependent adult requires the document that proves it.

8. Your rights

You can exercise at any time your rights of access, rectification, erasure, objection, restriction of processing and portability, and withdraw any consent without affecting what was done before. Almost everything is done from the app itself:

  • See and correct your data: in your record.
  • Take it with you: My account → Download my data (a ZIP with your record in FHIR, the international format for clinical reports, and the original reports).
  • See who has accessed it: in the access log.
  • Object to the verification of professionals (if you are a healthcare professional): you can object at any time by writing to the privacy email; without verification you will not be able to receive records as a professional (art. 21 GDPR).
  • Withdraw consents: My account (Mi cuenta) → My consents (Mis consentimientos). The consent to process your health data is what makes the app work: withdrawing it is the same as deleting the account.
  • Delete the account: My account → Delete my account, or at https://juancarjim.github.io/vitapass/borrar-cuenta/.

For anything else, write to [PENDING: privacy email] or to the data protection officer. We will reply within one month at the latest. If you disagree with the answer, you can complain to the Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD, C/ Jorge Juan 6, 28001 Madrid, www.aepd.es) or to the authority of your country.

Please note that we cannot recover your health data if you lose all your devices and your recovery key: it is encrypted with a key we do not have.

9. Security

  • End-to-end encryption of all clinical content, with keys generated on your devices.
  • App lock with Face ID, fingerprint or passcode, and the screen hidden in the app switcher.
  • Row-level security in the database, tested automatically.
  • Servers in the European Union, encryption in transit and at rest.
  • No internal tool allows viewing a person's data.
  • Data protection impact assessment carried out before launch, and a procedure to notify the AEPD within 72 hours and the people affected in case of a breach.

10. Changes to this policy

If we change it, we will tell you in the app and, if the change affects what you consented to, we will ask you to accept it again before continuing. Previous versions are available on request.

11. What VitaPass is not

VitaPass does not replace your official medical record or give medical advice: it stores and shows the information you provide. In an emergency, call 112.
